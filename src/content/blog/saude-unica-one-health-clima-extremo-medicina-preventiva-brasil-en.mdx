For decades, preventive medicine revolved around three familiar clinical pillars: nutrition, regular physical activity, and routine blood panels. In 2026, however, physicians, epidemiologists, and public health planners across Brazil face an external risk factor they can no longer overlook—**extreme weather**. Prolonged heatwaves, severe droughts, and torrential storms have become direct triggers for heart attacks, kidney injury, and infectious outbreaks in major Brazilian cities.

Findings from [The Lancet Countdown](https://www.lancetcountdown.org/), produced with active research contributions from Brazil's [Fiocruz](https://portal.fiocruz.br/) and federal universities, show that heat-related mortality in Brazil has **quadrupled** compared with historical baselines. Across South America, annual deaths linked to extreme heat have surpassed **13,000**, falling disproportionately on adults over 65, children, and patients living with chronic cardiovascular or respiratory conditions. In response, the **One Health** framework has moved to the center of preventive medicine in Brazil.

> **Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know**
> - **Rising Heat Mortality:** Heat-related deaths in Brazil have quadrupled according to *Lancet Countdown* indicators, while Fiocruz studies link tens of thousands of SUS cardiovascular and respiratory fatalities to prolonged heat exposure.
> - **Integrated Surveillance:** **One Health** connects human medicine, veterinary monitoring, and environmental data to anticipate dengue surges and zoonotic spillover.
> - **Clinical Adaptation:** Preventive protocols now include heatwave hydration plans, seasonal medication checks, and vector protection as cities warm.

## Thermal Stress Inside Brazilian Cities

When ambient temperatures stay elevated for days—especially inside urban heat islands where nighttime lows fail to cool—the human body faces nonstop physiological strain. The heart increases cardiac output to push blood toward the skin, heavy sweating depletes plasma volume, and thicker blood elevates risks of **stroke, thrombosis, arrhythmias, and acute kidney stress**.

Fiocruz research programs such as *Ciência&Clima* and *ProAdapta*, analyzing decades of public health records under Brazil's Unified Health System (SUS), demonstrate a clear statistical link between prolonged heat spells and tens of thousands of cardiovascular and respiratory deaths. Beyond clinical harm, *Lancet Countdown* tracking highlights billions of potential work hours lost across Brazil's agriculture and construction sectors.

## Climate-Sensitive Vectors and the One Health Imperative

Climate stress in Brazil extends well beyond heatstroke. Shifting rainfall patterns and warmer nights expand seasonal breeding windows for *Aedes aegypti*, boosting transmission suitability for dengue and other arboviruses even in regions that historically experienced cooler winters.

That overlap explains why **One Health** is replacing siloed emergency responses with cross-sector surveillance:

1. **Predictive Ecosystem Monitoring:** Combining deforestation alerts in the Amazon and Cerrado, water-quality metrics, and urban heat mapping to forecast vector spread and pathogen *spillover*.
2. **Water and Sanitation Resilience:** Severe floods and droughts disrupt potable water access, elevating risks of leptospirosis and waterborne infections.
3. **Unified Pathogen Control:** The same cross-disciplinary integration driving vector surveillance also anchors Brazil's response to [superbugs and antimicrobial resistance](/en/blog/resistencia-antimicrobiana-superbacterias-vigilancia-fiocruz-anvisa-brasil).

## How Preventive Care and Daily Habits Are Adapting

As Brazilian health outlets including [Folha de S.Paulo](https://www1.folha.uol.com.br/equilibrioesaude/) and [Estadão](https://www.estadao.com.br/saude/) report, climate awareness is now entering routine medical appointments. Cardiologists, geriatricians, and family doctors increasingly tailor seasonal guidance for vulnerable patients:

- **Medication Reviews During Heatwaves:** People taking diuretics, blood-pressure drugs, or certain psychiatric medications require medical guidance on fluid and electrolyte balance during prolonged heat alerts.
- **Safer Exercise Windows:** Shifting outdoor workouts before 9 a.m. or after 6 p.m. sharply lowers thermal cardiovascular strain.
- **Spotting Early Heat Exhaustion:** Dizziness, mild confusion, dark urine, or cramps in older adults during humid heat spells require rapid cooling and clinical evaluation.
- **Vaccination and Mosquito Control:** Keeping immunizations current and eliminating standing water after heavy storms remain high-impact preventive measures.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does the One Health approach mean?
One Health is an integrated framework endorsed by the WHO and Fiocruz that treats human health, animal health, and ecosystem stability as interconnected systems to prevent infectious outbreaks and climate-related illness.

### Why do heatwaves raise the risk of heart attacks and strokes?
Prolonged heat forces the heart to pump harder while fluid loss concentrates the blood, amplifying strain on blood vessels and kidneys—especially in older adults and people with hypertension.

### How can people protect their health during severe heatwaves in Brazil?
Clinicians advise steady hydration even before thirst sets in, avoiding strenuous outdoor activity during peak sun hours, keeping indoor spaces ventilated, and checking with a doctor if taking diuretics or blood-pressure medication.
