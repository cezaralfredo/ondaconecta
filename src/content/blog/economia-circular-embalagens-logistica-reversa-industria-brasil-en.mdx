For years, packaging recycling in Brazil leaned heavily on voluntary industry pledges and waste-picker cooperatives, without uniform legal accountability for every bottle or pouch placed on supermarket shelves. In 2026, that landscape shifted decisively: circular packaging moved from corporate sustainability brochures into **auditable federal law**.

The turning point is **Decree No. 12,688/2025**, which established binding reverse-logistics rules under Brazil's National Solid Waste Policy (PNRS). Beginning in **January 2026**, large manufacturers were required to incorporate **at least 22% post-consumer recycled (PCR) plastic** into packaging—followed by phased deadlines for small and mid-sized firms starting in July 2026—alongside escalating national recovery targets anchored at an initial **30%** baseline.

> **Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know**
> - **Binding 2026 Rules:** Decree No. 12,688/2025 requires manufacturers, importers, and retailers to verify reverse logistics through registered management entities and **Recycling Credit Certificates (CCR)**.
> - **Mandatory PCR Content:** Large companies in Brazil must include at least **22% recycled resin** in plastic packaging as of January 2026.
> - **Global Warning:** The **Ellen MacArthur Foundation** stresses that recycling alone will not solve plastic waste; upstream redesign and tackling flexible pouches are urgent priorities.

## Inside Decree 12,688 and Brazil's Recycling Credit Market

The federal regulation closed longstanding loopholes by covering **primary, secondary, and tertiary packaging**, plus single-use disposables, holding manufacturers, importers, distributors, and retailers jointly accountable.

To prove compliance to Brazil's Ministry of the Environment (MMA), firms can run individual take-back systems or join collective producer responsibility organizations. Traceability scales through **Recycling Credit Certificates (CCR)**, which channel verified funding directly to waste-picker cooperatives and sorting centers for every ton of material returned to industrial production. This push toward auditable compliance mirrors how Brazilian companies are adapting to [verified carbon accounting and ESG audits](/en/blog/fim-do-esg-de-planilha-carbono-balanco-auditado).

## The Flexible Packaging Bottleneck and Ellen MacArthur Insights

Despite Brazil's regulatory leap, global reports from the [Ellen MacArthur Foundation](https://www.ellenmacarthurfoundation.org/) underscore a sobering reality for consumer goods brands: **we cannot recycle our way out of plastic pollution**.

Across Latin America, the toughest technical hurdle remains **small-format flexible plastics**—sachets, multi-layer pouches, and snack wrappers. Lightweight, mixed-polymer, and costly to sort, these formats rarely enter conventional recycling streams and account for significant leakage into waterways.

To close that gap, the foundation advocates **upstream innovation** built on three practical shifts:

1. **Eliminating Excess:** Stripping away secondary plastic wraps, unnecessary seals, and plastic display windows on cardboard boxes.
2. **Scaling Reuse and Refill:** Expanding in-store refill stations and standardized returnable containers across home-care and personal-care aisles.
3. **Mono-Material Eco-Design:** Replacing unrecyclable multi-layer films with 100% mono-material polyethylene or polypropylene, or responsibly sourced paper barriers.

## What Changes on Store Shelves and Supply Chains

Throughout 2026, Brazilian consumer goods brands have accelerated **eco-design** overhauls across food, beverage, personal care, and household cleaning lines. Securing long-term supply agreements for food-grade PCR resin, upgrading automated optical-sorting facilities, and standardizing mono-material pouches have become top capital priorities to balance volatile virgin-versus-recycled resin costs while avoiding environmental penalties.

For shoppers, the transition is increasingly visible on supermarket shelves: bottles with slightly softer, matte recycled finishes, reduced label adhesives, concentrated formulas, and wider refill-pouch selections that cut plastic weight per use while lowering household waste.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does Brazil's Decree No. 12,688/2025 require for packaging?
It establishes mandatory reverse-logistics targets for packaging and single-use items under the PNRS, enforcing minimum recovery rates, post-consumer recycled plastic quotas, and verification via Recycling Credit Certificates (CCR).

### What is the minimum recycled plastic content required in 2026?
Since January 2026, large companies must incorporate at least 22% post-consumer recycled (PCR) resin in plastic packaging, with phased compliance starting in July 2026 for smaller enterprises.

### Why does the Ellen MacArthur Foundation say recycling alone is not enough?
Because small and multi-layer flexible plastics are rarely economically viable to recycle. Solving plastic waste requires upstream redesign, eliminating unnecessary packaging, and scaling reuse and refill models.
