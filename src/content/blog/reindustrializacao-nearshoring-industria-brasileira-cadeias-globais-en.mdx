Trade friction between the United States and China, alongside supply-chain shocks of recent years, has pushed multinationals to redraw global manufacturing maps. Rather than concentrating production solely in Asia, corporations are shifting factories toward closer, geopolitically aligned regions—a trend known as **nearshoring** and **friendshoring**. In that global realignment, Brazil is pitching its renewable power grid and vast domestic market as a launchpad for industrial renewal.

Domestically, the anchor of this push is **Nova Indústria Brasil (NIB)**, structured around six strategic missions through 2033 and executed largely through BNDES-backed credit lines. Figures compiled by Brazil's [National Confederation of Industry (CNI)](https://www.portaldaindustria.com.br/) show that between 2023 and mid-2026, roughly **R$ 755.2 billion** in financing backed more than **610,000 industrial projects** nationwide—targeting productivity (R$ 507.7B), innovation (R$ 82.5B), decarbonization (R$ 53.5B), and exports (R$ 51.2B).

> **Key Takeaways: What You Need to Know**
> - **Capital Surge:** Industrial policy channeled R$ 755.2 billion into more than 610,000 Brazilian projects between 2023 and 2026, according to CNI data.
> - **Selective Nearshoring:** Unlike Mexico—which captures US-bound assembly under the USMCA—Brazil competes in high-value niches like bioeconomy, green energy, digital agribusiness, and healthcare.
> - **Structural Drag:** Business groups such as **Fiesp** and **CNI** warn that *Custo Brasil* (estimated at up to R$ 3.5 trillion/year) and high real interest rates still erode factory competitiveness.

## Where Brazil Gains Ground in Global Value Chains

Unlike Mexico, which absorbed massive waves of light and automotive assembly aimed straight at US consumers, Brazil's entry into nearshoring follows a more selective path. European and Asian investors increasingly view Brazil as a **low-carbon manufacturing hub (*green friendshoring*)**.

Three verticals lead investment appetite:

1. **Energy Transition and Bioeconomy:** With one of the world's cleanest power grids, Brazil attracts energy-intensive manufacturers seeking low-carbon inputs to bypass strict environmental border tariffs in Europe.
2. **Healthcare Industrial Complex:** Post-pandemic supply security has spurred local production of pharmaceuticals, vaccines, and medical devices to cut reliance on distant imports.
3. **Agribusiness 4.0 and Mobility:** Automotive, aerospace, and agricultural machinery clusters linked to biofuels remain resilient regional exporters across Latin America.

## The Drag of Custo Brasil: Warnings From CNI and Fiesp

Despite substantial BNDES credit injections, Brazil's manufacturing sector still confronts steep structural headwinds. As highlighted by [Valor Econômico](https://valor.globo.com/), [Fiesp](https://www.fiesp.com.br/), and **CNI**, subsidized machinery loans solve only half the equation.

Recent industry estimates put the annual burden of **Custo Brasil**—the combined weight of tax complexity, bureaucracy, legal uncertainty, logistics bottlenecks, and energy costs—at up to **R$ 3.5 trillion per year**. Paired with elevated real interest rates in commercial lending, these frictions squeeze domestic margins against imported goods.

Consequently, industry leaders tie long-term reindustrialization to smooth implementation of Brazil's tax reform and faster upgrades to ports, railways, and highways. This push for operational scale also mirrors rising [M&A consolidation among mid-sized Brazilian companies](/en/blog/fusao-aquisicao-ma-empresas-familiares-reforma-tributaria-brasil).

## Opportunities for Mid-Sized Local Suppliers

For small and mid-sized Brazilian manufacturers, industrial expansion creates immediate demand across Tier-2 supply chains—from factory automation, robotics integration, and predictive maintenance to sustainable packaging and wind-and-solar components. Firms that digitize shop-floor operations, standardize quality certifications, and prove environmental traceability stand best positioned to enter demanding global supply networks.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is nearshoring and how does it impact Brazil?
Nearshoring is the corporate shift of factories and suppliers to geographically closer or politically aligned countries. In Brazil, it primarily attracts investment in clean energy, bioeconomy, agribusiness, and strategic industrial inputs.

### How much industrial credit has been deployed under Nova Indústria Brasil?
Data compiled by CNI shows that between 2023 and mid-2026, roughly R$ 755.2 billion in financing supported over 610,000 projects focused on productivity, innovation, green transition, and exports.

### What are the main barriers flagged by CNI and Fiesp?
Industry groups point to Custo Brasil (tax complexity, red tape, and logistics gaps), high commercial interest rates, and stiff competition from imported manufactured goods.
