Commercial aviation worldwide is standing before its most formidable technological crossroads since the invention of the jet turbine engine. Responsible for approximately 2.5% of anthropogenic carbon dioxide emissions, civil aviation cannot rely on heavy commercial battery banks for long-haul transatlantic routes, nor on hydrogen-powered airliners, which remain decades away from certified commercial maturity. The only realistic, drop-in pathway to achieve net-zero flight emissions by mid-century is **Sustainable Aviation Fuel** (**SAF**).

Within this geopolitical and ecological transformation, Brazil has established an unrivaled strategic advantage. Bolstered by the formal regulatory enactment of the **National Sustainable Aviation Fuel Program (ProBioQAV)** via Decree No. 13,094/2026—under the landmark **Fuel of the Future Law (Law No. 14,993/2024)**—the country has introduced binding greenhouse gas emission reduction mandates for airlines beginning in 2027, turning its century-old sugarcane and biofuels agro-industry into the planet's primary bio-jet fuel engine.

### In a Few Lines: Key Takeaways

- **Progressive Mandates Starting 2027:** ProBioQAV legally requires commercial air carriers operating domestic flights to reduce their greenhouse gas emissions by at least 1% starting January 1, 2027, scaling progressively to a mandatory 10% reduction by 2037.
- **The Alcohol-to-Jet (ATJ) Breakthrough:** Embracing strict technological neutrality, the Brazilian framework enables ethanol derived from sugarcane and sustainable corn crops to be converted into aviation kerosene boasting life-cycle emission reductions of up to 80% compared to fossil jet fuel.
- **Innovative Book and Claim Architecture:** The creation of the SAF Sustainability Certificate (CS-SAF) allows airlines to trade verified environmental attributes without requiring expensive physical logistics to secondary regional airports, solving distribution friction.

## The Brazilian Paradigm: Life-Cycle Emission Abatement Over Rigid Volume Mandates

Unlike foreign regulatory regimes—such as the European Union’s ReFuelEU Aviation framework, which enforces rigid volumetric blending quotas in each fuel tank—Brazil's Ministry of Ports and Airports, alongside the National Petroleum Agency ([ANP](https://www.gov.br/anp/pt-br)) and the National Civil Aviation Agency ([ANAC](https://www.gov.br/anac/pt-br)), opted for an engineering-first benchmark: **full life-cycle emission reduction efficiency**.

This distinction is crucial: instead of forcing carriers to pour a static percentage of alternative fluid into fuel manifolds, the law incentivizes the lowest possible carbon intensity across the entire production chain—from satellite-verified farming, zero-burn harvesting, and biorefinery cogeneration to airborne combustion:

```
[Brazilian Ethanol-to-Jet Value Chain]
   ├── Sustainable Feedstock ──► Certified sugarcane & off-season corn with geo-traceability
   ├── Advanced Biorefinery ──► Alcohol-to-Jet (ATJ) conversion powered by bagasse biomass
   ├── Quality & Verification ─► ANP oversight harmonized with ICAO CORSIA standards
   └── Jet Turbine Combustion ─► Up to 80% life-cycle net carbon emissions abatement
```

This performance-based metric directly benefits Brazil’s agricultural efficiency, which yields ethanol with one of the lowest carbon footprints on Earth. Major integrated energy conglomerates including Raízen, FS Bioenergia, and state-backed Petrobras are already mobilizing billions of dollars in capital expenditures to construct commercial-scale Alcohol-to-Jet processing plants throughout the country.

## Book and Claim Logistics and Alignment with ICAO's CORSIA

Historically, the most severe economic impediment to sustainable aviation has been terminal logistics. Trucking dedicated batches of bio-jet fuel to dozens of remote regional airports across continental territory creates substantial transport emissions and unsustainable distribution overheads.

To eradicate this barrier, Decree No. 13,094/2026 formalized the **SAF Sustainability Certificate (CS-SAF)**, operating on the globally recognized **Book and Claim** mechanism:

1. **Centralized Fuel Injection:** Biorefineries can inject their certified SAF directly into major pipeline manifolds feeding high-volume international hubs, such as São Paulo-Guarulhos (GRU) or Rio de Janeiro-Galeão (GIG).
2. **Environmental Attribute Decoupling:** While the physical green molecules blend smoothly into the broader jet fuel grid, verifiable digital emissions certificates (CS-SAFs) are registered on the ANP/ANAC platform.
3. **Seamless Regulatory Compliance:** A carrier refueling aircraft in regional hubs like Manaus or Curitiba can purchase CS-SAFs originating from Guarulhos to fulfill its statutory decarbonization quotas.

Furthermore, the Brazilian standard is comprehensively harmonized with **CORSIA** (*Carbon Offsetting and Reduction Scheme for International Aviation*), supervised by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO). As CORSIA enters its mandatory international phase in 2027, global carriers flying routes out of Brazil will be able to utilize Brazilian certified SAF to satisfy both domestic statutory mandates and international treaty obligations.

## Market Realities: Price Parity and Production Scaling

Despite extraordinary structural momentum, the aviation bio-economy must still conquer a pivotal economic challenge: production parity. Currently, sustainable aviation fuel trades at two to three times the price of conventional fossil Jet-A1 kerosene. Without careful transitional stewardship, this green premium could exert upward pressure on consumer airfares.

Recognizing this market dynamic, Brazilian regulatory authorities incorporated statutory market-stabilization mechanisms, granting administrative flexibility in the rare event of severe short-term physical supply shortages. Nevertheless, industry analysts forecast that the colossal feedstock scale and low cost of Brazilian ethanol will allow domestic producers to achieve competitive pricing significantly faster than European synthetic e-fuels or competing used cooking oil (HEFA) pathways.

By linking world-class tropical agriculture with high-precision aerospace manufacturing represented by giants like Embraer, Brazil is demonstrating that the green transition is not an economic penalty, but rather an engine for long-term industrial sovereignty.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is ProBioQAV and when does it take effect?
ProBioQAV is Brazil's National Sustainable Aviation Fuel Program enacted under the Fuel of the Future Law. It establishes mandatory greenhouse gas reduction targets for air carriers starting at 1% on January 1, 2027, and scaling progressively to 10% by 2037.

### Do commercial passenger aircraft require engine modifications to use SAF?
No. SAF is a fully compliant "drop-in" fuel engineered to meet rigorous international specifications (such as ASTM D-1655). It can be directly blended with conventional Jet-A1 without requiring modifications to existing aircraft engines, wings, or airport refueling infrastructures.

### How does the Book and Claim SAF accounting system work?
Book and Claim uncouples physical fuel delivery from its environmental sustainability value. Producers inject SAF into a centralized, efficient logistics pipeline, while airlines anywhere in the country can purchase the corresponding verified certificates (CS-SAF) to prove their carbon reduction compliance without shipping fuel across long overland distances.
